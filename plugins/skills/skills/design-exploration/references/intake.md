# Intake question bank

Pick what's still unknown after you've read the user's own sources. Ask 1–4 at a time, with
options where you can. Put your recommendation first, and use a preview for visual questions.
Stop asking once you can write the brief; later rounds will surface the rest.

## Purpose and audience
- What should this do for you? (Get to know me / get hired / sell a service / show the work / other)
- Who reads it, in order of importance? What should each understand in 30 seconds, and what
  would make them stay 5 minutes?
- Who is explicitly *not* the audience? (This prevents a sales tone, for example.)

## Content
- What has to be on it? (Offer an inventory from what you found in their sources: projects,
  roles, numbers, photos.)
- Which items carry the most weight? Is one story told properly, or many cards?
- What do you have in images: photos of yourself, screenshots, product shots? What would you
  shoot if asked?
- Which facts can't be public? (Client names, hosts, IPs, private repos, device models.)

## Look and feel
- Three words for the mood. (Offer pairs: calm/energetic, light/dark, editorial/technical,
  playful/serious.)
- One or two sites you like, and what exactly you like. One you dislike, and why.
- Anything that must or must not appear? (Brand colours, a font they love, an emoji they hate.)
- How "techy" should it look? (Plain and well-made / subtle nods / full concept.)

## Voice
- How do you talk? Pick one sample paragraph out of 3–4 voices you write for them
  (plain, playful, personal, short).
- Language(s)? Spelling conventions?

## Constraints
- Stack or hosting preferences (static site, framework, CMS, self-hosted)?
- Accessibility, performance or browser requirements?
- Is the source public? (Then the repo is part of the work and must hold up too.)

## Asking well
- Prefer options over open questions. "Which of these four describes your week?" gets better
  answers than "describe your week".
- When an answer needs a fact you can't verify, ask for the fact itself, not for permission to guess.
- Quote the user's own words back in the brief, so they recognise it as theirs.
